How to read the two scores

Value is the main score. Bottom in? is the timing check.

Value (0–100) shows how cheap BTC is against past cycle lows. Bottom in? (0–100) shows whether the fall has stopped. Read them together. Each card shows how its score is made: points that add up to the number. Value alone acts too early: in 2022 it first read Cheap in June at about $22,300, then price fell a further 31% to $15,479. Bottom in? alone acts too late: it reached 50 two to three months after each past low, when price was already 27–90% above it.

      How the scores work

      Value is the main score. It answers one question: is BTC cheap against past cycle lows? Each of 16 signals gets a score from 0 to 100. The score goes up as the signal gets closer to the level it reached at past lows. The value score is the weighted average. If a reading is missing, its weight goes to the others.

      Small-cycle levels. Each past low was less deep than the one before. For six signals, the full-score level moves with that trend. Switch to Classic to see the score with the old, fixed levels.

      Bottom in? is the timing check. It asks a different question: has the fall stopped? It looks for six signs that only come after a low: no new low for weeks, a bounce, price back above recent buyers' cost and the 200-day average, selling at a profit again, and miners recovered. It stays low until the value score has reached 75 at least once in this bear market, because no past low came before that. Money flows are shown but not scored. The price checks under it (depth, higher low, trend) are not scored either: they show what has improved and the price that would undo it.

      Read them together. Value alone acts too early: cheap prices can get cheaper. Bottom in? alone acts too late: it confirms a low after price has already risen. Both are scores, not probabilities.
